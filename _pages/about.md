I'm **Weiping Yan (闫维平)**. I am now an Electrical Engineering and Computer Science student in the [University of Minnesota College of Science and Engineering](https://cse.umn.edu/); [Fall 2026 classes started on September 8, 2026](https://asr.umn.edu/2026-27-twin-cities-and-rochester-calendar). Before that I studied Computer Science & Engineering, Electrical Engineering, and Applied Physics side by side at [Delft University of Technology](https://www.tudelft.nl/en/) and [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://www.tue.nl/en/), and took part in the [TU/e Honors Academy — Honors Bachelor Program](https://educationguide.tue.nl/programs/honors-academy) and the [TU Delft Honours Programme Bachelor (HPB)](https://www.tudelft.nl/en/student/eemcs-student-portal/education/honours-programme) in 2025–2026 before transferring.

I'm interested in [artificial intelligence](https://scholar.google.com/citations?view_op=search_authors&hl=en&mauthors=label:artificial_intelligence), [natural language processing](https://scholar.google.com/citations?view_op=search_authors&hl=en&mauthors=label:natural_language_processing), [microelectronics](https://scholar.google.com/citations?view_op=search_authors&hl=en&mauthors=label:microelectronics), and [electronic design automation](https://scholar.google.com/citations?view_op=search_authors&hl=en&mauthors=label:electronic_design_automation), and most of all in where they meet: using language models to help design and check analog circuits.

Since July 2026 I have been an NLP research intern with the [Minnesota NLP Group](https://nlp.umn.edu/), where I build tools that record and compare how people and computer-use agents work through the same browser tasks. Before that I was a research assistant at The Chinese University of Hong Kong (LLM-based recommendation) and at Zhangjiang Laboratory (the analog IC design flow), and a teaching assistant for calculus and logic at Eindhoven University of Technology.

I also like fixing problems in the open-source tools I use; [20 of my pull requests](/repositories/) have been merged into projects such as OpenRAM, CharLib, Power Grid Model, PISA, and LensKit. My first paper, written in high school, analyzed PM2.5 exposure and respiratory disease on synthetic data and appeared in _Theoretical and Natural Science_. It was an independent student project, not a position at Carnegie Mellon University.
